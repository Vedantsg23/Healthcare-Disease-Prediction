        Medicine guideUse medicines safely

        Six habits that keep everyday medicines safe, and a quick reference for the common ones.

        Read the label

        Read the pack and leaflet before the first dose. Check the expiry date and how to store it.

        Keep the timing

        Some medicines go with food, others on an empty stomach. Never skip a dose or take two at once.

        Prescription first

        Many medicines here need a doctor’s prescription. Treating a serious condition yourself can be dangerous.

        Why it matters

        Watch interactions

        Tell your doctor about everything you take. Some combinations cause side effects or cancel each other out.

        Drink water

        Unless told otherwise, take medicines with plenty of water so your body can absorb them properly.

        Children and older people

        Doses are often different. Ask a paediatrician or a doctor who knows the person’s other medicines.

        FAQGood to know

        Questions,
        answered

        Is this a diagnosis?

        No. MediSense is an educational tool. It points you to conditions worth reading about, but only a doctor who examines you can diagnose you.

        How does MediSense pick a condition?

        Each condition in the library has a set of typical symptoms. MediSense compares your picks with those sets and shows the closest match and how well it fits.

        Where do my symptoms go?

        Nowhere. The symptom checker runs in your browser and nothing is saved. Only messages you type to the assistant are sent to the server, to get an answer.

        What can the assistant help with?

        It answers general questions about conditions, symptoms and medicines in plain words, and always reminds you to check with a doctor.

        Can I take the medicines listed?

        Not without advice. The lists show medicines doctors commonly use. Many need a prescription, and the right one depends on you.

        Feeling unwell for more than a day?

        Talk to a doctor.
